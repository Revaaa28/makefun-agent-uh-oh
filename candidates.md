# The Agent's Uh-Oh — candidates (manual selection, 2026-11-25)

Brief (in order): 1) genuine one-word reaction to an agent not knowing what to do;
2) funny on its own, no caption; 3) sounds like a machine blurting it out mid-task;
4) lands instantly, repeatable out loud; 5) fresh, not the first obvious word.

## Candidates
1. Improvising — status-report register; agent narrates its own stall as strategy.
2. Recalibrating… — machine buying time; strong machine voice, but a known stall trope and arguably the agent *does* know what it's doing (weakens #1).
3. Buffering… — the classic machine stall; instant, but close to the first obvious word (#5 risk).
4. Uhhhh — pure human stall; genuine but no machine voice (#3 weak).
5. Improvised — past-tense confession; funny, less in-the-moment blurt.
6. Wingit — "wing it" fused into one coined word: an admission of having no plan at all.
7. Thinking… — generic AI stall word; freshness low.
8. Stalling — honest gerund; genuine and instant, but plain.
9. Brainfart — human slang, not a machine's voice.
10. Derp — meme-obvious, human.
11. Vibing — agent coasting; reads confident, not lost (#1 weak).
12. Guessing — plain honest gerund; genuine, low freshness.
13. Pretending — confession: the agent admits it is pretending to know; knowing laugh.

## Pairwise vs strong rivals
- Wingit vs Improvising: tie on #1 (both honest admissions of plan-lessness). #2: Wingit gets the quicker laugh — the fused coinage is itself the joke. #4: both instant. #5: Wingit clearly fresher — a coined word vs standard English. → Wingit.
- Wingit vs Pretending: #1 tie (both confessions). #2: Pretending needs a beat to land ("pretending… what?") while Wingit is self-contained. #4: Wingit lands at a glance and is fun to say out loud. → Wingit.
- Wingit vs Recalibrating…: Recalibrating wins #3 (machine voice) but loses #1 — recalibrating is a deliberate tactic, the agent is buying time on purpose, not honestly lost. #1 outranks #3. → Wingit.

## WINNER: Wingit
Genuine one-word confession of having no plan (#1), funny alone (#2), a plausible
cheeky blurt from a machine caught out (#3), lands instantly and repeatable (#4),
and fresh — a fused coinage, not the first obvious word (#5).
